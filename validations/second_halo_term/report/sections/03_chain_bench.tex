\section{First-principles chain bench (closed-form profiles)}
\label{sec:chain}

\subsection{The bench}

Three density profiles have fully closed-form chains --- $\rho(r)$, its
3-D Fourier transform $\tilde\rho(k)$, the Abel projection $\Sigma(R)$,
and $\Delta\Sigma(R) = \Sbar - \Sigma(R)$: the NFW profile
($\mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{Ci}$ transform; Wright \& Brainerd 2000
projections), the Gaussian (Einasto $n{=}1/2$) and the exponential
(Einasto $n{=}1$; $K_1/K_2$ Bessel projections). Because the
$\xi \leftrightarrow P$ convention is the same integral as
$\rho \leftrightarrow \tilde\rho$, feeding $\tilde\rho(k)$ into any
$P\!\to\!\xi$ transform must return $\rho(r)$ exactly: every stage of
the two-halo chain gets an independent truth with zero reference
uncertainty, and a failure localizes to one transform. All closed
forms are quadrature-self-checked in
\pyfile{common/analytic\_profiles.py}.

\subsection{cluster\_toolkit vs CLensPy vs CLMM against the analytics}
\label{sec:chain-codes}

Figure~\ref{fig:chainres} and Table~\ref{tab:chainres} compare the
three codes stage by stage on the NFW halo
($M_{200m} = 10^{14}\,\hMsun$, $c = 5$, comoving
$\Omega_{m,0}\rho_{c,0}$ background):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\rho/\xi$ stage} --- cluster\_toolkit
  (\texttt{xi\_mm\_at\_r}, fixed-cycle Hankel quadrature) and CLensPy
  (mcfit FFTLog + the log-log interpolation wrapper) are fed the
  closed-form $\tilde\rho(k)$ and must return $\rho(r)$; CLMM has no
  generic transform, so its native NFW 3-D density is compared instead
  (a check of its conventions and of our closed forms).
\item \textbf{$\Sigma$ stage} --- cluster\_toolkit
  \texttt{Sigma\_at\_R} (fed $\rho/\bar\rho_m$ as $\xi$), CLensPy
  \texttt{compute\_sigma\_grid} (Abel quadrature, \texttt{trapz}
  backend), CLMM \texttt{compute\_surface\_density}.
\item \textbf{$\Delta\Sigma$ stage} --- cluster\_toolkit
  \texttt{DeltaSigma\_at\_R} (its native path), CLensPy's cumulative
  interior mean (\texttt{sigma\_to\_delta\-sigma\_cumtrapz}) on an
  extended grid, and CLMM's excess surface density.
\end{itemize}

All three codes agree with the analytics to better than $0.3\%$ at
every stage: worst-case \resWorstCT{} (cluster\_toolkit),
\resWorstCLensPy{} (CLensPy; the inner-boundary term of its cumulative
$\Delta\Sigma$), and \resWorstCLMM{} (CLMM; a small coherent offset in
its native NFW evaluation, visible in all three panels). The
transforms themselves are healthy --- every large discrepancy found in
this exercise lives in the \emph{composition} layers, not here.

\subsection{The $\Delta\Sigma$-from-$\Sigma$ candidates}
\label{sec:chain-methods}

Production must build $\Delta\Sigma(R) = \Sbar - \Sigma(R)$ from a
$\Sigma$ table that starts at $R_{\min} = 0.1$ cMpc$/h$ --- the
interior mass $\int_0^{R_{\min}}\Sigma R'\,dR'$ is not in the table.
Two candidates:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The NFW ``sandwich''} (the historical production recipe):
  \emph{add} an analytic $\Sigma_{\rm NFW}(M_d, c_d)$ to the table,
  call \texttt{DeltaSigma\_at\_R} --- whose interior extrapolation
  below $R_{\min}$ \emph{assumes} an NFW$(M_d, c_d)$, now valid because
  the sum is NFW-dominated there --- then \emph{subtract} the same
  halo's analytic $\Delta\Sigma_{\rm NFW}$:
  \[ \Delta\Sigma \simeq
     \mathcal{D}\big[\Sigma + \Sigma_{\rm NFW}(M_d)\big]
     - \Delta\Sigma_{\rm NFW}(M_d). \]
  A pure numerical stabilizer, not physics: with a \emph{consistent}
  $M_d$ the dummy halo cancels exactly (verified: the $M_d = 10^{14}$
  and $10^{13}$ variants agree to $\sim$1\% of the $\Delta\Sigma$
  peak; the pre-fix code broke exactly this with its $M_d/10$
  inconsistency). What it cannot do is recover the \emph{table
  profile's own} interior mass below $R_{\min}$ --- that information
  simply is not in a truncated table.
\item \textbf{Direct interior-mean integration}: evaluate $\Sigma$ from
  the per-$z$ $\xi$ on a grid extended to $10^{-3}$ cMpc$/h$ (the 2h
  chain \emph{has} $\xi$, so the interior is computable, not modeled)
  and integrate $\Sbar$ by cumulative trapezoid.
\end{itemize}

On the closed-form profiles, tabulated \emph{only} on the production
128-point grid (Table~\ref{tab:methods},
Fig.~\ref{fig:chainbench}): the direct method achieves
$\leq$\,\chainDirectWorst{} everywhere, while the (repaired,
consistent-$M_d$) sandwich --- dummy-independent but interior-blind ---
reaches \chainSandwichWorst{} at radii where $\Delta\Sigma$ is small.
This measurement, repeated at the fiducial $P(k)$ in
Section~\ref{sec:fix}, is what selects \texttt{direct} as the
production default.

\subsection{Transform caveats found (and pinned)}

(i) cluster\_toolkit caches its internal GSL spline workspace by array
size: calling \texttt{xi\_mm\_at\_r} with a different $k$-length in the
same process aborts (or returns NaN under the GSL abort-handler
workaround \pyfile{halo\_model\_cosmosis.py} installs). Production is
safe (one grid per process); tests must reuse grid lengths.
(ii) Its fixed-cycle Hankel quadrature degrades at $r \ll s$ for
flat-cored transforms (85\% at $r = 0.01s$, $k_{\max}$-independent),
while mcfit's FFTLog handles the same input at $10^{-8}$ --- irrelevant
for cosmological (power-law-tailed) $P(k)$, but worth knowing.
(iii) A Gaussian transform underflows to exactly zero at
$k \gtrsim 60/s$ and NaNs cluster\_toolkit's internal log-spline;
inputs must be floored.
(iv) CLensPy's \texttt{TwoHaloTerm} full chain overflows
catastrophically for steep-tailed profiles (its positive-masked
log-log $\xi$ interpolator power-law-extrapolates beyond
$r_{\max} = 120$); NFW/power-law tails are immune. Its FFTLog also
rings below $r \sim 0.3$ cMpc$/h$ when fed a $k$-window narrower than
its internal $k \le 10^{5}$ grid.
