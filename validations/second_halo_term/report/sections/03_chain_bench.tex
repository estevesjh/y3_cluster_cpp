\section{First-principles chain bench (closed-form profiles)}
\label{sec:chain}

Three density profiles have fully closed-form chains --- $\rho(r)$,
$\tilde\rho(k)$ (3-D Fourier transform), $\Sigma(R)$ (Abel projection),
and $\Delta\Sigma(R)$:
NFW ($\mathrm{Si}/\mathrm{Ci}$ transform; Wright \& Brainerd 2000
projections), the Gaussian (Einasto $n{=}1/2$,
$\rho \propto e^{-r^2/2s^2}$ with $r_0 \approx 1$ Mpc as the flat-core
case) and the exponential (Einasto $n{=}1$, $K_1$/$K_2$ Bessel
projections). Since the $\xi \leftrightarrow P$ convention is the same
integral as $\rho \leftrightarrow \tilde\rho$, feeding
$\tilde\rho(k)$ into any $P\!\to\!\xi$ transform must return
$\rho(r)$ exactly --- each stage gets an independent truth with zero
reference uncertainty. All closed forms are quadrature-self-checked in
\pyfile{common/analytic\_profiles.py}.

\paragraph{Stage results.}
$P\!\to\!\xi$: cluster\_toolkit's \texttt{xi\_mm\_at\_r} recovers the
production-like (power-law-tailed) NFW transform to
$1.8\times10^{-4}$; mcfit's FFTLog is machine-precision
($\sim10^{-8}$) on every input.
$\xi\!\to\!\Sigma$: \texttt{Sigma\_at\_R} $\leq 5\times10^{-4}$ and
CLensPy's Abel quadrature $\leq 4\times10^{-5}$ on all profiles.
$\Sigma\!\to\!\Delta\Sigma$ is where all the structure lives
(Table below, Fig.~\ref{fig:chainbench}): the \emph{direct}
extended-grid interior-mean integration achieves
$\leq$\,\chainDirectWorst{} everywhere, while the (repaired,
consistent-$M_d$) NFW sandwich is dummy-halo-independent but loses the
profile's own interior mass below the table edge --- up to
\chainSandwichWorst{} at radii where $\Delta\Sigma$ is small.

\paragraph{Transform caveats found (and pinned).}
(i) cluster\_toolkit caches its internal GSL spline workspace by array
size: calling \texttt{xi\_mm\_at\_r} with a different $k$-length in the
same process aborts (or returns NaN under the GSL abort-handler
workaround \pyfile{halo\_model\_cosmosis.py} installs). Production is
safe (one grid per process); tests must reuse grid lengths.
(ii) Its fixed-cycle Hankel quadrature degrades at $r \ll s$ for
flat-cored transforms (85\% at $r = 0.01s$, $k_{\max}$-independent),
while mcfit's FFTLog handles the same input at $10^{-8}$ --- irrelevant
for cosmological (power-law-tailed) $P(k)$, but worth knowing.
(iii) A Gaussian transform underflows to exactly zero at
$k \gtrsim 60/s$ and NaNs cluster\_toolkit's internal log-spline;
inputs must be floored.
(iv) CLensPy's \texttt{TwoHaloTerm} full chain overflows
catastrophically for steep-tailed profiles (its positive-masked
log-log $\xi$ interpolator power-law-extrapolates beyond
$r_{\max} = 120$); NFW/power-law tails are immune.
