\section{The $\xinl$ table: production audit and the linear-$P(k)$ gap}
\label{sec:xinl}

$\xinl(r, z)$ is the single matter-correlation input of the
selection-affected two-halo formalism: the paper's operators
$I_2 \equiv \mathcal{P}[b\,\xinl]$ and
$I_1 \equiv \mathcal{P}[b\,\xinl\,\sigma(\theta)]$, and the
$\Sigma^{\rm prj}$/$\Delta\Sigma^{\rm prj}$ projection integrals, all
consume it. This section audits where it is built, where it is
consumed, whether each link is numerically correct, and what physics it
currently contains.

\subsection{Where it is built and consumed}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Producer} ---
  \pyfile{halo\_model\_cosmosis.py} (\texttt{execute}, one line): per
  redshift slice, the cluster\_toolkit Hankel transform of
  $P_{\rm nl}(k, z_i)$ on $r \in [10^{-3}, 10^{3}]$ cMpc$/h$ (128 log
  points, 50 redshifts), published as \blk{xi\_nl}. Note this loop
  indexes $P(k, z_i)$ \emph{correctly} --- the issue-\#4 z-degeneracy
  bug lived only in the \blk{Sigma\_hh} path.
\item \textbf{$P_{\rm nl}$ source} --- the block section
  \blk{matter\_power\_nl} if present; otherwise a silent fallback
  aliases the \emph{linear} $P(k)$
  (\pyfile{halo\_model\_cosmosis.py:132-137}).
\item \textbf{Consumer: $\bsel$ wall} ---
  \modname{BSelMargIntegrand.so} (\pyfile{p\_operator\_t.hh}) reads the
  table into a flat array with a hand-rolled bilinear lookup and
  evaluates $(P_1, I_1, J{=}I_2{-}I_1)$ per wall row
  (redshift-bin bounds $\times$ richness bin);
  \pyfile{bsel.py} then turns the wall into the $\bsel(\theta)$
  sigmoid.
\item \textbf{Consumer: projection} --- the three \texttt{sp\_detail}
  cores in \pyfile{sigma\_prj\_t.hh} (\modname{SigmaPrjEvaluator},
  \modname{DSigmaPrjEvaluator}, \modname{ShearPrjFrozenPhysics}) read
  it as an \texttt{Interp2D} and evaluate $\xinl(\Delta\chi, \zob)$,
  $\Delta\chi^2 = \chi_z^2 + \chi_o^2 - 2\chi_z\chi_o\cos\theta$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is verified correct}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The transform}: the dump's \blk{xi\_nl} table matches a
  converged per-$z$ recomputation of the same input $P(k)$ to
  \xiNlDevMax{} over $r \in [0.5, 50]$ cMpc$/h$ at all comparison
  redshifts, and the four-way $\ximm$ comparison
  (cluster\_toolkit / CLensPy-FFTLog / pyccl) agrees to
  \xiMutualMax{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:xi4way}).
\item \textbf{Consumer units}: both consumers convert
  $\chi$ to cMpc$/h$ ($\times h_0$;
  \pyfile{sigma\_prj\_t.hh:415, :530}) before querying, matching the
  table's $r$ axis; the grid $[10^{-3}, 10^{3}]$ covers every
  $\Delta\chi$ the integrals request, so the \texttt{clamp} never
  actually extrapolates.
\item \textbf{Consumer evaluation redshift}: the table is queried at
  $\zob$ (the observed cluster redshift), consistent with the
  cylindrical two-halo specialisation used in the paper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{$\xi$ from the cp\_camb emulator vs CAMB, across
cosmologies}
\label{sec:xinl-emulator}

Production's only $P(k)$ source is the \modname{cp\_camb} linear
emulator, so its error propagates directly into $\xinl$. For the
fiducial cosmology and one-at-a-time variations in
$\Omega_{m,0}$, $\sigma_8$ and $h$ (all inside the training box), the
emulator's $P(k, z{=}0)$ and the $\ximm(r)$ transformed from it were
compared against real CAMB at matched $\sigma_8$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:xiemu}): the worst $\xi$ residual over
$r \in [0.3, 50]$ cMpc$/h$ across all six cosmologies is
\xiEmuWorst{} --- negligible against every other budget in this
report. The residual is dominated by smoothing of the BAO wiggles
($k \sim 0.1$--$1\,h/$Mpc) and stays flat in $r$ until the
zero-crossing region. The growth factor is applied identically to the
emulator and CAMB spectra downstream (massless neutrinos, so the
scaling is scale-independent); $z = 0$ therefore isolates the pure
emulator error.

\subsection{The physics gap: $\xinl$ contains linear $\xi$}
\label{sec:xinl-gap}

The paper's model prescribes $\xinl$ from the nonlinear (halofit)
power
spectrum. In practice \blk{matter\_power\_nl} has never existed: the
\modname{cp\_camb} emulator ships only linear models
(\texttt{camb\_linear\_*.npz}), no production ini sets its
(already-implemented) \texttt{nonlinear\_pk\_path} option, and the
fallback silently substitutes the linear $P(k)$. Every $\bsel$,
$\Sigma^{\rm prj}$, $\Delta\Sigma^{\rm prj}$ and $\DShh$ result to date
has therefore used $\xi_{\rm lin}$ under the \blk{xi\_nl} name.

Measured at the fiducial cosmology, $z = 0.41$ (CAMB NL, Takahashi
2012, vs linear): the ratio $\xi_{\rm NL}/\xi_{\rm lin}$ reaches
\xiNlLinRatioROne{} at $r = 1$ cMpc$/h$, and
$\DStwo^{\rm NL}/\DStwo^{\rm lin} = \dsNlLinRatioRThree$ at
$R = 3$ cMpc$/h$, converging to unity only for $r \gtrsim 20$ cMpc$/h$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:xinlfallback}). The correlated-structure terms are
underestimated by these factors at small and intermediate separations
(modulated by $\bsel$ and the radial weighting of each observable).

\textbf{Agreed path}: keep the linear $P(k)$ for now (all current
validation and mock work is internally consistent and documented as
linear); add the nonlinear input by updating \modname{cp\_camb} with a
CAMB NL emulator --- the module and the fallback then propagate it to
$\xinl$, $\DShh$ and the whole projection branch with zero further code
changes. The CAMB NL (Takahashi 2012) reference for the acceptance test is
already
generated (\pyfile{outputs/pk\_camb.npz}, key \texttt{p\_k\_nl}).

Two consequences: (i) the $(P_1, I_1, J)$ wall produced by

even though it is the direct carrier of $\xinl$ into $\bsel$; (ii) the
projection chain's only cross-check is a port of the same algorithm,
so a method-level error (grid placement, kernel convention) would pass
undetected. The recommended follow-up is a dump-fed adaptive-quadrature
reference for the wall (the RichnessSelection \texttt{quad\_truth}
pattern re-pointed at the dump's own $\xinl$/HMF/bias/selection
tables), plus a genuine \texttt{full\_ltmz}-class reference for
projection.

\subsection{Current vs the CAMB NL input at the consumer level}
\label{sec:xinl-impact}

The $P(k)\to\xi$ chain fix (issue \#4) is validated against CLensPy
at every stage (Sections~\ref{sec:chain} and \ref{sec:postfix});
what remains is the \emph{input} upgrade. To measure what the planned
CAMB NL input changes \emph{in the observables}, the
full consumer chain (\modname{cp\_camb} $\to$ \modname{halo\_model}
$\to$ \modname{b\_sel\_marg} $\to$ \modname{bsel} $\to$
\modname{DSigmaPrjEvaluator} $\to$ \modname{ShearPrjFrozenPhysics})
was run twice with identical configuration
(\pyfile{validations/second\_halo\_term/xinl\_consumers.ini}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{current} --- linear $P(k)$ under the \blk{xi\_nl} name
  (the silent fallback; what every run to date has used);
\item \textbf{CAMB NL} --- Takahashi-2012 nonlinear
  \blk{matter\_power\_nl}
  injected (\pyfile{common/inject\_pk\_nl.py}, the harness CAMB
  reference), everything downstream untouched. Sanity:
  the resulting $\xinl$ table ratio at $r = 1$ cMpc$/h$, $z = 0.41$ is
  \xiTableNlLinAtROne{}, matching the standalone prediction.
\end{itemize}

These runs required closing the bsel wall-metadata contract gap with a
harness-local shim (\pyfile{common/bsel\_wall\_meta.py}); they are the
first end-to-end executions of the refactored $\bsel$ chain.

\textbf{$\bsel(\theta)$, marginalized over $\lambda^{\rm tr}$}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:bseltheta}): the CAMB NL $\xinl$ \emph{lowers} the
small-scale plateau substantially --- $b_s$ from \bSmallCurrent{} to
\bSmallProposed{} (\bSmallShift) in the lowest richness bin at
$\zob = 0.425$ --- while the large-scale plateau moves by only a few
percent ($b_\ell$: \bLargeCurrent{} $\to$ \bLargeProposed). The
selection-bias \emph{contrast} shrinks because the boosted small-$r$
correlation raises the unselected expectation against which the
selection excess is measured.

\textbf{$\Delta\Sigma^{\rm prj}(R)$} (Fig.~\ref{fig:dsprj}): the two
effects largely cancel in the observable --- the CAMB NL input shifts
$\Delta\Sigma^{\rm prj}$ by at most \dsPrjMaxShift{} (a $-9\%$
suppression at $R \lesssim 0.3$ cMpc$/h$ turning into a $+3\%$
enhancement near $R \approx 2$, and $\lesssim 1\%$ beyond
$R \sim 5$). The observable-level impact of the linear-$P(k)$ gap is
therefore far milder than the raw factor-\xiNlLinRatioROne{} $\xi$
ratio suggests --- but it is scale-dependent and coherent across bins,
i.e.\ shaped like a signal, which is exactly why the nonlinear input
should land before cosmology inference.
